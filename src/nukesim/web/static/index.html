      What if a nuke exploded underwater

      See what a nuclear burst over (or under) the sea does to the water and to the fish, whales, divers and boats in it, with every number taken from published sources.

      1. Pick an example at the top, or set the burst yourself on the left.
      2. Add things to the sea and click one to see what happens to it.
      3. Use the timeline at the bottom to play the event from the flash to weeks later.
      4. Switch to Realistic to watch it as it would look. Press F to fly around freely.
      5. Basic / Expert switches how much detail you see.

      An educational tool, not a substitute for detailed engineering models. All numbers carry the limits of their sources.

      How does this work?

      This is a simulator, not a game engine: every distance, pressure, dose and time comes from published science about nuclear weapons and the sea, mostly The Effects of Nuclear Weapons (Glasstone & Dolan, 1977), U.S. Navy studies of explosions and marine animals, and reports from the 1946 Crossroads tests at Bikini Atoll.

      • The sizes are real. The fireball, the cloud, the blast front and the waves are drawn at the size the models give.
      • The verdicts come from injury criteria measured in tests: how much pressure hurts a fish's swim bladder, a dolphin's lungs, a person's ears.
      • Some things are only drawn, like the shape of the cloud's puffs, how bodies tumble, and the spray. The app says which in Expert mode.
      • It is an educational tool. Real effects depend on many things it can't know, and it is not a substitute for detailed engineering models.
